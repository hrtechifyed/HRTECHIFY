AI + WorkPerspective 01~90 sec read

Will AI take my job—or just change what my job is?

A repetitive question. An understandable fear. And probably the wrong question.

AI now appears in almost every business conversation. A dramatic prediction lands, a CEO warns that jobs may disappear, and the headline travels faster than the context. Fear is easier to sell than evolution.

But work has always changed with technology. Transportation replaced some occupations and created drivers, mechanics and logistics networks. Computers removed large amounts of clerical work while creating software, cybersecurity and digital industries.

AI will be no different in one important sense: some tasks will disappear, some jobs will change, and new work will emerge.

Ask a better question

That does not mean every role survives. It means the useful question for individuals is not, “Will my job exist in five years?” but:

What will make me valuable if my job changes?

Look at your own work. Which tasks are repetitive? Which depend on judgment, trust, context, creativity or understanding another human being? What can technology now handle—and what should you learn so you can move toward higher-value work?

Leaders face the same choice

A weak AI strategy asks, “How many jobs can this remove?” A stronger one asks, “How should work be redesigned now that this capability exists?”

Automation can create efficiency, but it can also create capacity: better customer experiences, less burnout, more time for managers to coach, and more room for people to solve harder problems.

AI should change work. That is the point. The challenge is making sure people are not simply changed out of it.

Work evolves. The people and organizations most likely to remain relevant are the ones willing to evolve with it.

So perhaps the question is not whether AI will take your job. It is: how will you keep creating value when the job itself changes?
